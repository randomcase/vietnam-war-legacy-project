# Outline

I. Introduction
   A. Hook: the Paris Peace Accords stopped the fighting and did not end the war's effect on human lives.
   B. Background: the air campaign (7.6 million tons), the herbicides (19 million gallons), the prisoners (591 returned; nearly 800 captured).
   C. Thesis.

II. The immediate human return: Operation Homecoming, 1973
   A. 591 prisoners in 54 flights, February to April; the Freedom Birds out of Gia Lam.
   B. The rift: celebration of the 591 against the 2,646 then listed missing.
   C. Transition: the prisoners were welcomed; the sick were not believed.

III. The domestic battle: Agent Orange
   A. The invisible wound: dioxin, and the children of the exposed.
   B. Institutional resistance: VA denial; the 1984 settlement ($180 million); the Zumwalt report (1990); the Agent Orange Act (1991).
   C. Expansions: Blue Water Navy Act (2019); PACT Act (2022). The Vietnamese exposed have no act.

IV. Healing the ground: UXO clearance
   A. The threat: 30% failure rate; 80 million submunitions in Laos; 20,000 Lao and 100,000 Vietnamese casualties since 1975.
   B. The technology: drones, LiDAR, thermal imaging, magnetometers, GPR, the THOR bombing database cross-referenced by AI.
   C. The people: local technicians, many of them women; farmers reporting by phone; risk education by song; land back to coffee and pepper.

V. Conclusion
   A. Thesis restated.
   B. The three lifespans reconciled: the prisoners' trauma, the veterans' health, the cleared fields.
   C. Final thought: the timeline of a war is defined by the work of healing it, and that work is a century long in Laos.

Alternative titles for III by tone: "Institutional Liability and the Legislative Evolution of Toxic Exposure Compensation" (academic); "The Invisible Poison" (narrative); "Combat Beyond the Battlefield" (argumentative).
