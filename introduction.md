# Introduction (argumentative tone)

The conventional timeline of the Vietnam War says the conflict ended with a signature on the Paris Peace Accords in January 1973, or with the fall of Saigon in April 1975. That framework is wrong, because it stops the clock where the fighting stopped and ignores the decades of domestic and humanitarian battles fought after it. The real conclusion of the war spans half a century of activism and engineering: American veterans forcing their own government to admit what dioxin did to them, and Lao and Vietnamese villagers, with drones overhead and detectors in hand, taking their fields back from the ordnance left in them. The war is not a closed chapter. It is an open, generational process of repair, and the timeline of a war is set not by its declarations of peace but by the work required to heal what it did.
